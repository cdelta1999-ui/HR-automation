02 / THE VOICE

The email suite

Four templates that carry the whole journey. Every one is short, mobile-first, written like a person, and built with {{merge tokens}} so automation stays personal. Each closes with why it’s written the way it is.

Template 01 · Application Received · auto-sent < 2 min
Subject
Your application to {{Company}} is in, {{FirstName}}
Preview text: Here’s exactly what happens next — no black box.

Hi {{FirstName}},

Thanks for applying for the {{RoleTitle}} role. Your application is safely with us, and a real person — with a little help from our tools — is reviewing it now.

Here’s what to expect:

  • Within {{X business days}} you’ll hear whether we’re moving to a first conversation.
  • Either way, we’ll email you. We don’t leave applications unanswered.
  • Questions in the meantime? Ask our assistant any time at {{FAQLink}} or just reply here.

The road ahead is usually a short screening call, a conversation with the team, then a decision — around {{2–3 weeks}} start to finish.

Thanks for your interest in {{Company}}.
— {{RecruiterName}}, Talent Team

Why it works: Sent instantly, so the candidate never wonders if it went through. It sets a concrete timeline (the #1 cure for applicant anxiety), promises a reply either way, and offers self-serve answers — deflecting “any update?” emails before they’re written.

Template 02 · Interview Invite
Subject
Let’s talk — {{RoleTitle}} at {{Company}}
Preview text: Pick a time that works for you.

Hi {{FirstName}},

Good news — we’d love to talk with you about the {{RoleTitle}} role.

Grab whatever time suits you here: {{SchedulingLink}} (30 minutes, video).

You’ll be meeting {{InterviewerName}}, {{InterviewerTitle}}. We’ll cover your background, the role itself, and leave plenty of room for your questions.

To help you prepare:

  • {{PrepResourceLink}} — the team, the product, and what this role owns.
  • Bring questions. This is a two-way conversation, not an interrogation.

Need to reschedule, or have any accessibility requirements? Just reply — we’ll sort it.

Looking forward to it,
— {{RecruiterName}}

Why it works: Self-scheduling removes days of back-and-forth. Naming the interviewer lowers pre-interview nerves, the prep link levels the playing field, and the explicit accessibility invite signals an inclusive employer from the first real touch.

Template 03 · Rejection · post-interview, personalized
Subject
An update on your {{RoleTitle}} application
Preview text: A note from the team, and an open door.

Hi {{FirstName}},

Thank you for the time and energy you put into interviewing for the {{RoleTitle}} role — meeting you was a genuine pleasure.

After a lot of thought, we’ve decided to move forward with another candidate for this position. It was a close call, and it isn’t a reflection of your ability.

{{OptionalFeedback}} — one honest, specific, kind line where you can offer it.

We’d like to stay in touch. With your permission, we’ll keep your details on file and reach out when something that fits comes up. You can also watch open roles here: {{CareersLink}}.

Wishing you the very best in your search — truly.
— {{RecruiterName}}

Why it works: It’s prompt and unmistakably human — no cold boilerplate. It thanks the candidate specifically, offers real feedback where possible, and keeps the door open, which protects the employer brand and feeds a warm talent pool. (An early-stage variant is shorter and softer, but follows the same reply-either-way promise.)

Template 04 · Offer Extension
Subject
We’d love for you to join {{Company}}, {{FirstName}}
Preview text: The details, and a time to talk it through.

Hi {{FirstName}},

It’s official — we’d love to offer you the {{RoleTitle}} role at {{Company}}. The whole team is excited about what you’ll bring.

The headlines:

  • Role: {{RoleTitle}}, reporting to {{ManagerName}}
  • Start date: {{StartDate}} (flexible if you need it)
  • Compensation: {{Salary}} + {{Equity/Bonus}} + {{BenefitsSummary}}

Everything to review and sign is here: {{OfferLink}}.

Let’s find 15 minutes to walk through it together and answer anything — no pressure, we want you to feel great about this: {{SchedulingLink}}.

Take the time you need. We’re here for every question.

Welcome (almost!),
— {{RecruiterName}} & the {{Company}} team

Why it works: Leads with warmth, then puts the key terms right up front so the candidate isn’t hunting through a PDF. A live walkthrough plus one-click e-sign lowers friction, and “take the time you need” respects the decision — which lifts acceptance rates.

03 / THE ENGINE

Automation & AI proposal

Two AI capabilities carry the volume — a résumé screen and an FAQ concierge — wrapped in automation that removes waiting and administrative drag. The principle throughout: the machine handles scale; a human owns every judgment about a person.

Capability A

AI résumé screening

Parses and scores applications against the role’s success profile, ranks a shortlist, and flags borderline cases for human review. It recommends — it never auto-rejects a strong-adjacent candidate on its own.

Rollout in three phases

Phase 1Weeks 1–4

Foundation — close the silences

  • Build the ATS pipeline stages and the reply-either-way rule into workflow.
  • Ship the four email templates and wire the instant Application Received trigger.
  • Turn on self-scheduling and automated interview reminders.
Phase 2Weeks 5–8

Intelligence — pilot the AI

  • Pilot AI résumé screening on one or two high-volume roles, with recruiters auditing every recommendation.
  • Launch the FAQ concierge with a curated question set and a human-handoff path.
  • Calibrate scoring thresholds and baseline the bias audit.
Phase 3Weeks 9–12

Scale & optimize

  • Stand up the drop-off dashboard and review it weekly.
  • A/B test subject lines and timings against candidate NPS and response rates.
  • Expand AI screening to more roles under a written governance policy.

Guardrails — keeping the human element

  • Human owns the decision. AI ranks and recommends; a recruiter confirms or overrides. Borderline profiles always route to a person.
  • Audited for bias. The model scores against role criteria only, never protected characteristics or their proxies, with regular adverse-impact review.
  • Transparent to candidates. Applicants are told automation is used and can reach a human at any point.
  • Privacy by default. Consent-based data handling, clear retention limits, and GDPR/CCPA alignment.
  • No cold auto-rejections. Every decline — even an early one — is a written, warm message, timed with a short human-feeling delay.
